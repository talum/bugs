Ode to New York City

By the Hudson's steady flow,
Where bridges arc and bright lights glow,
A city rises, proud and tall,
With room enough to hold us all.

Manhattan lifts her towers high,
Glass and steel that kiss the sky,
A skyline etched in memory,
A silhouette of history.

The Brooklyn Bridge at morning light,
Its cables silver, stretched and tight,
Walkers cross on wooden planks
And offer up their quiet thanks.

In Central Park the joggers run
Beneath the late October sun,
While children laugh on grassy knolls
And poets stroll with restless souls.

The subway hums beneath the street,
A million strangers chance to meet,
From Harlem down to Battery Park,
From golden noon to winter dark.

Times Square erupts in neon streams,
A carnival of restless dreams,
Where tourists stop to gape and stare
At light and sound that fill the air.

In Chinatown the lanterns sway,
And vendors call the hours away,
The scent of jasmine, char, and spice
Turns every corner into paradise.

The Bronx, where hip-hop found its voice,
Where Yankee fans still dare rejoice,
Where corner bodegas never sleep
And grandmothers their vigils keep.

On Staten Island, ferries glide
Across the harbor, wide and wide,
The Lady Liberty stands tall,
Her torch a beacon held for all.

In Queens a hundred languages
Are spoken over market stalls,
Where Elmhurst hums with Flushing's beat
And Jackson Heights perfumes the street.

The High Line floats above the freight,
A garden built from industry's fate,
Where wildflowers thread through rusted rail
And artists tell their modern tale.

At Coney Island summers bloom,
The Cyclone rattles, riders zoom,
And children race the rushing tide
On boardwalks stretching far and wide.

The Cloisters crown the northern hill,
Where medieval silence fills
The arched colonnades and stones
With echoes of forgotten thrones.

Harlem's brownstones, row on row,
Keep memories of an age aglow,
Of Langston, Miles, and Billie's song
That proved the night was far too long.

Washington Square sees students pass
With coffee cups and blades of grass,
While chess players in the fountain's shade
Map out the moves that kings have made.

The Chelsea galleries at dusk
Hold canvases of paint and musk,
Where art leaps free from every frame
And nothing twice looks quite the same.

The Fulton fish stalls greet the dawn,
Their silver catches laid and drawn,
As chefs arrive before the light
To claim the sea's most precious night.

The Plaza holds its amber glow
As horse-drawn carriages below
Clip-clop their way through autumn leaves
While Fifth Avenue never grieves.

The East River catches fire
When sunsets set the clouds alight,
And tugboats push against the tide
As office towers bid good night.

In Astoria the coffee steams,
The tavernas pour their honey streams,
And old men argue, laugh, and toast
The city that they love the most.

Along the Gowanus, murals blaze,
A testament to better days,
Where neighbors turned an empty lot
Into a garden—why not?

The libraries hold centuries
Of thought in quiet galleries,
The lion statues flank the door
And dare you come to learn once more.

St. Patrick's spires pierce the haze
Of taxis caught in midday blaze,
A prayer in stone among the rush
Of commerce born from push and shush.

Grand Central's ceiling blooms with stars,
Commuters hustle, missing cars,
The whisper gallery holds a word
Breathed softly still to be still heard.

The whole of earth has sent its best—
Its dreamers, fighters, dispossessed,
To plant their flags on this hard ground
And turn their losses all around.

New York, you are no single thing,
No borough, street, or song to sing,
You are the sum of every voice
That ever dared to make a choice.

You are the immigrant's first view
Of something old made wholly new,
The child who rides the train alone,
The elder walking slow toward home.

You are the night that never ends,
The argument that always bends
Toward something better, something more,
A city worth the fighting for.

So here I stand on your worn stone,
And know I'll never be alone,
For eight million souls have lit this place,
And left their light in every face.

New York, you are the world in brief,
A map of joy, a map of grief,
And still you rise with every dawn—
Impossible, and carrying on.
